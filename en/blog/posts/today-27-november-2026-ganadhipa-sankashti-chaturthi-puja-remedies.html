Today's Special Festival: Ganadhipa Sankashti Chaturthi

In India, the fast of Ganadhipa Sankashti Chaturthi is being observed today, Friday, November 27, 2026. Sankashti Chaturthi occurs every month on the Chaturthi Tithi of the Krishna Paksha and is dedicated to Lord Ganesha. On this day, there is a tradition of worshipping Lord Ganesha in his Ganadhipa form. Ganadhipa means the lord or leader of the ganas. Therefore, this day is regarded as an opportunity to focus on decision-making ability, wisdom, discipline and the inner strength needed to deal with obstacles.

Some people may be confused about the date because, according to the panchang for Delhi, the Tritiya Tithi continues until approximately 9:48 a.m. on November 27, after which the Chaturthi Tithi begins. The Chaturthi Tithi will continue until the morning of November 28. Sankashti fasting is generally determined by whether the Chaturthi Tithi is present at moonrise. For this reason, the main tradition of observing the fast and offering arghya to the Moon is being followed on November 27 itself.

Spiritual Significance of Sankashti Chaturthi

The word ‘Sankashti’ is associated with the feeling of relief from difficulties. The purpose of this fast is not only to remove external obstacles but also to reduce mental restlessness, haste, negative thinking and confusion in decision-making. Lord Ganesha is worshipped as the deity of intelligence, wisdom and new beginnings. Therefore, while performing spiritual practices on this day, it may be more useful to identify a genuine problem in your life and decide on practical steps toward resolving it.

The spirit of Ganadhipa Sankashti may involve reducing disorder in our lives, preparing a list of unfinished tasks and focusing on one important goal at a time. From an astrological perspective, the Moon is also regarded as an indicator of the mind and emotional state. With the Moon in Gemini, the speed of thoughts may remain high. In such circumstances, meditation, silence and organised communication may help maintain mental balance.

Simple Puja Method for Ganadhipa Sankashti Chaturthi

Those observing the fast may choose its form according to their health condition and family tradition. Some people observe a waterless fast, some consume fruits, while others have sattvic food. In case of a health problem, pregnancy, old age or regular medication, prioritise medical advice and a comfortable diet instead of undertaking a strict fast.

  • Take a bath in the morning, wear clean clothes and clean the place of worship.
  • Light a lamp before Lord Ganesha and offer durva grass, red or yellow flowers, fruits and an offering.
  • Offer modaks, jaggery and roasted gram, or another sattvic food according to your tradition.
  • Chant the mantra ‘Om Gam Ganapataye Namah’ with devotion. Give greater importance to regularity and concentration than to the number of repetitions.
  • You may recite the Ganesh Atharvashirsha, Ganesh Chalisa or the Sankashti fast story.
  • After moonrise, offer water, akshat and flowers to the Moon. Then complete the worship of Lord Ganesha and break the fast.

There is no religious compulsion to look directly at the Moon during the puja; arghya may be offered according to local tradition. Buildings or tall structures may delay the visibility of the Moon, so it is normal to wait a few minutes after the time mentioned in the panchang.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Should the Sankashti Chaturthi fast be observed on November 27, 2026?

Yes. In India, the Sankashti fast is being observed on November 27, 2026, because the Krishna Paksha Chaturthi Tithi will be present at moonrise. Moonrise and Tithi timings may differ slightly between cities, so it is advisable to consult the local panchang.

2. How should the Ganadhipa Sankashti fast be broken?

After moonrise, worship Lord Ganesha, offer arghya to the Moon and then break the fast with fruit-based or sattvic food according to your health condition. A waterless fast is neither necessary nor suitable for everyone.

3. Is it necessary to chant a specific mantra on this day?

No single mantra is mandatory. You may devotionally chant ‘Om Gam Ganapataye Namah’ or recite the Ganesh Atharvashirsha or Ganesh Chalisa. Keep your mantra practice according to your personal faith and convenience.
